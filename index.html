About

From coastal observations to satellite-scale insight

I am an oceanographer and doctoral researcher in the Bio-Optical and Satellite Oceanography Laboratory at the University of Georgia’s Skidaway Institute of Oceanography. My work spans ocean color remote sensing, satellite algorithm development and validation, marine biogeochemistry, water quality, and machine learning.

My current research includes hyperspectral approaches for estimating total suspended solids, evaluation of small-satellite ocean-color observations, and integration of in situ measurements with satellite imagery. I am especially interested in reproducible field-to-satellite workflows that support coastal science and management.

Research

Questions I work on

01

Satellite algorithms

Developing and validating algorithms for total suspended solids, chlorophyll-a, particulate organic carbon, and related coastal-water properties.

02

Ocean optics

Linking high-resolution optical observations with phytoplankton variability, sediment dynamics, and ocean-color signals.

03

Coastal processes

Studying water clarity, hydrography, circulation, and climate variability in the northern Bay of Bengal and the South Atlantic Bight.

04

Data-driven oceanography

Applying machine learning, reproducible computation, and field-to-satellite integration to environmental observations.

Teaching & mentoring

Learning by connecting concepts, data, and place

I have taught undergraduate oceanography and marine-science courses, including Introduction to Oceanography, Biological Oceanography, Marine Resources, Ocean Optics, Physical Oceanography laboratory, Fisheries Oceanography laboratory, and Oceanographic Cruise Techniques.

My teaching emphasizes active learning, reproducible analysis, field experience, visual communication, and mentoring students through research projects.
