About Me

I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Marine Sciences at the University of Georgia (UGA) Skidaway Institute of Oceanography under the supervision of Dr. Sara Rivero-Calle. My research specializes in ocean color remote sensing, bio-optical oceanography, physical-biological coupling, and coastal marine water quality dynamics.

I leverage multi-mission satellite imagery (PACE, MODIS, VIIRS, Sentinel-3, SeaHawk CubeSat), high-performance computing (HPC), machine learning, and in situ oceanographic observations to advance our understanding of phytoplankton productivity, suspended sediments, and carbon cycling in complex aquatic systems.

Research Interests

  • Ocean Color & Optical Oceanography: Hyperspectral & multispectral remote sensing (PACE, SeaHawk, MODIS, Sentinel-3).
  • Coastal & Shelf Processes: Physical-biological coupling across river plumes, fronts, eddies, and stratified systems.
  • Phytoplankton Dynamics: Ecology, biogeography, community variability, and primary productivity.
  • Marine Carbon Cycling: Cross-shelf exchange, total suspended solids (TSS), and carbon sequestration.
  • Data Integration & AI: Machine learning, agentic AI, High-Performance Computing (HPC), and in situ optical sensors.

Research Highlights

Bio-Optical Algorithms & Satellite Remote Sensing

Developing novel regional and global bio-optical algorithms to quantify Total Suspended Solids (TSS) and Chlorophyll-a in complex optically active waters (Case-2 waters) such as the South Atlantic Bight and the northern Bay of Bengal using NASA PACE OCI, Sentinel-3 OLCI, and SeaHawk CubeSat data.

Field Oceanography & Radiometry Closure

Participating in multi-day oceanographic cruises across the South Atlantic Bight, North Sea, Baltic Sea, and Bay of Bengal collecting CTD, HyperPro, SVC, fluorometer, and water quality data for satellite validation and matchup analysis.
